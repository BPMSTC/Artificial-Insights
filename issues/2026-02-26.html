Pentagon Pressures Anthropic as Safety Guardrails Shift

The final major AI company adhering to strict ethical guidelines has revised its position in response to the Trump administration demands. On Tuesday, Anthropic met with Pentagon leadership to discuss the use of Claude AI by the military related to their position refusing to allow Claude to be used for mass surveillance of U.S. citizens or fully autonomous weapons. During this same meeting, CEO Dario Amodei was informed that the Claude AI must be available for “all lawful purposes,” warning that failure to comply could result in the termination of contractual agreements, designation as a “supply chain risk,” or even action under the Defense Production Act—all of which would have likely been a company ending scenario. Following this meeting, Anthropic’s board of directors updated their Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) to remove the prohibition on these items. They maintain that the expectation is for them to continue to be the safest of the frontier models. I am concerned about this move, because Anthropic was created specifically to offset the concerns about OpenAI moving the business faster than the guardrails. As a whole, guardrails are being ignored more and more. I suspect that many of the key personnel at Anthropic will not like this decision, and may leave.

🔬 What Kind of Hard Is Your Problem?

AI capability is advancing across very different categories of difficulty. If we are designing curriculum, programs, and workforce education, we need to be clear about which "hard" we are preparing students for. There are four distinct types of hard work- each of which AI has varying amounts of effectiveness with. First, "Thinking Deep Hard" — complex, novel problem solving. AI is excellent at this. Items such as solving high-tier math problems, or business planning problems using unconventional approaches would fall into this category. Second, "Crunching Data Hard" — sustained, detail-oriented execution. Say we have to review 3,000 medical records, or reconcile 500 financial records, or scanning an entire codebase. AI does not get tired like we do, and doesn't make mistakes. This is something that AI is also excellent at. Third, "Relationship Vibe Hard" — judgment, taste, and accountability. These are things that most of us do every day such as providing difficult feedback, deciding to stop working on a long-term project, deciding how a customer feels about a decision. Anything where you don't have complete data. AI is terrible at this, and is not likely to improve anytime soon. This is where being human is an advantage over using AI.

The Lesson: The skills needed for program students will hit all three of these, but the real strength will be in the third area. Items one and two are foundational knowledge that must be there in order to utilize AI tools, but then the advantage we can give our students is the ability to do three. Ensure that they can frame a problem for AI, evaluate and verify outputs, and make the final human decision when trade-offs are real.

🎓 Stanford Study: "Tutor CoPilot" Boosts Real-Time Tutoring Effectiveness

Tutor CoPilot is an AI system designed to help human tutors in real time by suggesting responses that take into consideration the best practices in tutoring strategies. In this study, students working with tutors who used the system were more likely to master math topics. Gains were especially strong for students paired with less-experienced tutors. My take: While the gains were kind of modest (about 5%), this is the first study I've seen that pairs the AI tutoring tools with a tutor in this manner. Rather than asking whether AI can fully automate instruction, this study addresses helping students in a manner that seems more likely to be successful - by helping the tutor react in a way that will help the student the most.

🎬 NotebookLM: Editable Slides + Conversational Studio

NotebookLM is a tool that most of you have heard of, and you know that I love it. There are two upgrades I'd like to make you aware of. First, editable slide decks... you can now revise slides with prompts targeted at specific slides. Previously you had to re-do the prompt and simply try again. I love this feature so far. Second, conversational studio generations — you can produce different versions of outputs for different audiences. I think this is more targeted to say orientating employees in different departments, since they will need some different information for each department. For students, while this is cool, I've been struggling to think of a way to apply it.
